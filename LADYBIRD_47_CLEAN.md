## Important Notes

- It is a very useful practical-test shortlist.
- Where Ladybird wording needed tightening, the answer here leans toward current RSA wording.

## Key Corrections To Learn

- For **contra-flow bus lanes**, learn: buses only **unless signs also authorise cyclists**.
- For the **traffic-light sequence**, learn the full Irish sequence: `Green -> Amber -> Red -> Red+Amber -> Green`.
- For **hatched markings**, learn the conservative practical answer: `Do not enter the area they cover.`

### Overtaking

1. **When can you overtake on the left?**
   Answer: When the driver ahead has moved right and signalled right, when you have signalled left, or in slow-moving traffic where the left lane is moving faster.
2. **Where should you not overtake?**
   Answer: Near bends, hill crests, hump-back bridges, continuous white lines, entrances, taxi ranks, or where the road is narrow or visibility is poor.
3. **When being overtaken, what must you not do?**
   Answer: Increase your speed.

### Road Markings

4. **What is meant by a solid white line in the centre of the road?**
   Answer: Keep left and do not cross the line except for access or in an emergency.
5. **What does a broken yellow line mean?**
   Answer: It marks a hard shoulder, normally for pedestrians and cyclists; you may pull in briefly to let faster traffic pass if it is safe.
6. **What do double broken white lines along the centre of the road mean?**
   Answer: They warn that continuous white lines are ahead; do not cross unless it is safe.
7. **Where there is a continuous and a broken white line along the centre of the road, which one do you obey?**
   Answer: The line nearest to you.
8. **What are hatched markings?**
   Answer: Diagonal or chevron markings used to separate or guide traffic; you must not enter the area they cover.
9. **What are the road markings for no entry?**
   Answer: A continuous and a broken white line with the words NO ENTRY.

### Junctions

10. **At a stop sign that has no white line, where should you stop?**
   Answer: At the stop sign.
11. **What rules apply to a box junction?**
   Answer: Do not enter unless you can clear it without stopping; the main exception is turning right while waiting for a safe gap without blocking traffic with priority.
12. **What does a yield sign mean?**
   Answer: Give way to traffic with priority and do not proceed unless it is safe.

### Right Of Way

13. **Who has priority at a roundabout?**
   Answer: Traffic already on the roundabout.
14. **Who has priority at an unmarked crossroads with roads of equal importance?**
   Answer: Traffic from the right and traffic already on the junction.

### Traffic Lights And Visibility

15. **When driving, when should you dip your headlights?**
   Answer: When meeting traffic, following closely behind another vehicle, on continuously lit roads, and in poor visibility such as fog, snow, dusk, or dawn.
16. **What should you do if you are dazzled by another vehicle's headlights?**
   Answer: Slow down and stop if necessary; watch for pedestrians and cyclists; look towards the left verge if the dazzle is from oncoming traffic.
17. **What is the sequence of traffic lights?**
   Answer: Green, Amber, Red, Red+Amber, Green.
18. **What is a filter light?**
   Answer: A green or amber arrow that lets you proceed in the direction of the arrow if it is safe.
19. **You are approaching traffic lights and they change to amber. What should you do?**
   Answer: Stop unless it is unsafe to do so.
20. **What does a flashing amber traffic light mean?**
   Answer: Proceed with caution if the crossing or junction is clear; pedestrians have priority.
21. **What do flashing red traffic lights mean?**
   Answer: Stop; a train is approaching or the crossing is operating.

### Crossings

22. **What is the difference between a pelican crossing and a zebra crossing?**
   Answer: A pelican crossing is controlled by lights; a zebra crossing is controlled by pedestrian priority and flashing amber beacons.
23. **What does an island in the centre of a pedestrian crossing mean?**
   Answer: Treat each side of a zebra crossing as a separate crossing; a staggered pelican crossing also counts as two crossings.
24. **What do the white zig-zag lines at a zebra crossing mean?**
   Answer: No overtaking and no parking.
25. **Within what distance from a pedestrian crossing should you not park?**
   Answer: Within 15 metres before it or 5 metres after it.

### Speed Limits

26. **What is the speed limit on national roads and on motorways?**
   Answer: National roads are generally 100 km/h; motorways are generally 120 km/h unless signs say otherwise.
27. **What are the speed limits in built-up areas?**
   Answer: Usually 50 km/h unless a special limit such as 30 km/h or 60 km/h is posted.

### Technical Checks

28. **What is the minimum tread depth on tyres?**
   Answer: 1.6 mm over the central three-quarters of the tread.

### Bus Lanes

29. **What are the rules regarding bus lanes?**
   Answer: A with-flow bus lane runs with the traffic and can be used by buses, taxis, and bicycles during the stated times; a contra-flow bus lane is generally for buses only unless signs also authorise cyclists.

### Parking

30. **Within what distance from the kerb should you park?**
   Answer: Within 45 cm.
31. **What does a clearway mean?**
   Answer: No parking during the stated times and no stopping unless you are held up in traffic.
32. **How far away should you park from a junction?**
   Answer: At least 5 metres away.
33. **What does a continuous yellow line at the side of a road mean?**
   Answer: No parking during certain hours as shown.
34. **What do double continuous yellow lines at the side of the road mean?**
   Answer: No parking at any time.
35. **Give examples of where you would not park.**
   Answer: Near bends, hill crests, hump-back bridges, continuous white lines, entrances, narrow roads, taxi ranks, junctions, bus stops, or on a footpath.

### Positioning

36. **What position would you take up for a right turn in a one-way street?**
   Answer: As close as practicable to the right-hand side.

### Dual Carriageway

37. **What procedure must you adopt if crossing a dual carriageway with a narrow central median?**
   Answer: Wait until you can complete the whole crossing safely if the median is too narrow for your vehicle.
38. **When following the road ahead on a multi-lane dual carriageway, which lane would you normally use?**
   Answer: Lane 1, the left-hand driving lane, unless signs or road markings say otherwise.

### Motorway

39. **What are the differences between a motorway and a dual carriageway?**
   Answer: Motorways have special rules, left-side exits, no crossroads, no roundabouts, and no ordinary traffic lights; a 120 km/h limit may apply on motorways.

### Country Roads And Awareness

40. **What would you look out for on country roads?**
   Answer: Pedestrians, cyclists, animals, mud, concealed entrances, bends, and slow-moving farm machinery.

### Manoeuvres

41. **Where would you never make a U-turn?**
   Answer: In a one-way street, where continuous white lines apply, or where a sign prohibits it.

### Safe Driving

42. **What is meant by tailgating?**
   Answer: Driving too close to the vehicle in front.
43. **What human factors can affect your driving?**
   Answer: Alcohol, drugs, tiredness, fatigue, anger, attitude, and poor concentration.
44. **What is the 2 second rule?**
   Answer: A way to judge safe following distance: at least 2 seconds in dry conditions, at least 4 in wet conditions, and more in ice.

### Vehicle Control

45. **What is coasting and why is it potentially dangerous?**
   Answer: Coasting is driving with the clutch down or the gear lever in neutral; it reduces your control of the vehicle.

### Safety Rules

46. **Who is responsible for seat belts?**
   Answer: The driver is responsible for passengers under 17; passengers aged 17 and over are responsible for themselves.

### Signals

47. **What restrictions are there in relation to the use of the horn?**
   Answer: Do not use it in a built-up area between 11:30 pm and 7:00 am unless there is a traffic emergency.
